Deviations from the runbook's assumptions:

- Arch Linux instead of Ubuntu 24.04. Toolchain present: cmake 4.3.3, gcc 16.1.1, glslc 2026.2, glslang/vulkan-headers 1.4.350, shaderc, spirv-headers, ninja. Nothing installed; sudo needs a password.
- Model on a SATA SSD (860 EVO), because the NVMe has under 10 GB free. Expect slower cold-cache first prompts and slower SSD streaming on long prompts than the runbook assumes.
- CPU frequency governor is `powersave` (intel_pstate default, still boosts). Not changed.
- `iostat` (sysstat) is not installed; `vmstat`/`/proc/diskstats` used instead for the SSD-streaming check.
- **PCIe link is Gen 3 x8** (8 GT/s x8 between the B580's on-package switch at 01:00.0 and the Comet Lake root port 00:01.0; the switch itself is Gen 4 capable, the CPU is not). About 6.5 GB/s usable. The GPU's own function 03:00.0 reports an internal 2.5 GT/s x1 port, which is not the external link. nvtop shows `GEN 3@ 8x`.
- The desktop session holds about 1.2 GiB of VRAM before any server starts (10965 MiB free of 12216).
- `scripts/build.sh` needed one fix for `BACKEND=sycl`: oneAPI 2026.0's `setvars.sh` reads an unset variable (`OCL_ICD_FILENAMES`) and the script runs under `set -u`, so the first SYCL build aborted in 2 s; the script now relaxes `-u` around the `source`.
- `scripts/env.sh` gained one knob, `NCPUMOE` (default 99, unchanged behaviour), so that whole expert layers can be placed in VRAM with `--n-cpu-moe`; env.sh's hardcoded value would otherwise win because tensor overrides are first-match.

## Runs

Measure with the second of two identical requests, 256 tokens, temperature 0. One row per configuration.

Profile: `scripts/profile.sh` took 123 s; chat trace 27,397 rows (512 decode tokens), code trace 12,333 rows (the model hit end-of-text after about 197 tokens), merged 39,730 rows of which 34,464 are decode rows. The model routes 10 of 512 experts per layer per token and used about 357 distinct experts per layer in the traces. Coverage of decode routing by the hottest experts per layer: top-32 37.0%, top-48 47.3%, top-56 51.7%, top-64 55.7% (mean over layers; the least skewed layer is at 29% for top-48). So 48 slots move roughly half of the expert work onto the GPU.

### Recommended configuration on this machine

```bash
GGML_VK_DISABLE_GRAPH_OPTIMIZE=1 SLOTS=48 ASYNC_CPU=1 THREADS_BATCH=6 scripts/serve.sh -ub 2048
```

That is the cache at 48 slots, the async CPU scheduler, the Vulkan graph optimizer disabled (required for correct output with the cache), 6 decode threads and 6 batch threads (physical cores), and a 2048-token micro-batch. It decodes at about 8.6 tok/s from a short prompt and 11 tok/s after a long one, and prefills at about 400 tok/s. Add `USE_MTP=1` only for short-context chat: it lifts short-prompt decode to about 10 to 12 tok/s but its draft acceptance drops to zero after a long prompt, where it costs 40 percent of decode and 13 percent of prefill. Do not set `GGML_SCHED_PREFETCH_EXPERTS`. Do not go below `-t 6`/`-tb 6` or above them.

### Why prefill moved 16x and decode barely moved

- Decode is bound by the six Comet Lake cores computing the routed experts (all six saturated at 4.0 GHz, GPU at 30 percent). The cache moves 47 percent of expert routing into VRAM but Vulkan's single-token matmul over the hot pack plus the extra per-layer split costs almost as much as it saves: +8 percent with async, a loss without it. Whole expert layers in VRAM (`NCPUMOE=43`) and more slots (56, 60) do not change decode. Only MTP does, and only on short contexts.
- Stock prefill offloads every expert matmul to the GPU (the Vulkan backend offloads any mul_mat_id with 32 or more tokens) and the scheduler copies the experts each micro-batch uses into the GPU compute buffer: for a 512-token micro-batch that is nearly all 512 experts of every layer, about 45 GB per micro-batch, serialized with a GPU sync per weight tensor to read the routing ids. On PCIe Gen 3 x8 that gives the 20 to 26 tok/s observed. Disabling offload and running the experts on the CPU is faster (39 tok/s) but then bound by expert pages spilling out of the 45 GiB page cache to the SATA SSD.
- With the cache, the cold-chain ids mark the 48 hot experts of each layer as -1 and the scheduler's used-experts copy skips them, while the hot chain runs from VRAM; measured 186 tok/s at `-ub 512`. The scheduler dump (`results/logs/sched-dump-cache48.log`) confirms the graph shape: 147 splits at batch 512, expert weights listed as copied inputs, versus 145 in the baseline. Raising the micro-batch amortizes the per-micro-batch copy and sync cost: 186 / 347 / 401 / 390 tok/s at 512 / 1024 / 2048 / 4096 (rows 5, 17, 18, 25). Batch threads at 6 instead of 12 gave +41 percent at 512 (row 19).
- The fork's `GGML_SCHED_PREFETCH_EXPERTS` uploads whole expert tensors asynchronously; on this machine it touches all 44.7 GiB of expert pages per micro-batch, the page cache cannot hold them, and the SATA SSD saturates (rows 22 and 23). Not usable with 48 GB RAM.
- A per-op GPU timing run (`GGML_VK_PERF_LOGGER=1`) was attempted for both configurations to measure the offloaded expert matmuls directly; the server aborts with `ggml-vulkan.cpp:17600: GGML_ASSERT(ctx->compute_ctx.expired()) failed` right after the warm-up graph in both runs (`results/logs/perf-base.log`, `results/logs/perf-cache48.log`), so the per-op split between copy, sync and compute is not measured here. The only numbers it logged, from the batch-2 warm-up graph, show the hot-pack `MUL_MAT_ID iq2_s m=640 n=10 k=2560 n_expert=48` at about 0.8 ms per op, which is consistent with the cache's small decode gain: the GPU hot matmul at batch 1 is not much cheaper than the CPU work it replaces.
- VRAM: the desktop holds 1.2 GiB; the recommended configuration leaves about 1.8 GiB free at `-ub 2048` and 0.8 GiB at `-ub 4096`. Slots 48 to 60 all fit; none of the extra slots paid, so the spare VRAM is best left to the micro-batch compute buffer.

## SYCL

Built because oneAPI 2026.0 and the Level Zero runtime were already installed (`sycl-ls` lists the B580 as `level_zero:gpu`). `BACKEND=sycl BUILD_DIR=build-sycl scripts/build.sh` compiles the fork's SYCL tree unchanged once `setvars.sh` is sourced with `-u` relaxed.

- **Stock (no cache): SYCL is the faster backend on this machine.** Decode 9.50 tok/s vs 8.0 to 8.2 on Vulkan, 9.9 vs 7.1 after the long prompt, prefill 42.6 vs 24.6 tok/s at `-ub 512`, and its first-token probabilities are within 0.02 of the CPU reference where Vulkan's are 0.08 off. Rows 28, 30 and 31.
- **The static expert cache does not run on SYCL.** The pack uploads (`4347.66 MiB uploaded to SYCL0`) but the first graph aborts in the SYCL `mul_mat_id` row-mapping code, `ggml/src/ggml-sycl/ggml-sycl.cpp:4958`, on `GGML_ASSERT(row_id_i >= 0 && row_id_i < n_as)`. The fork's hot/cold split encodes "expert not in this pack" as id -1 (`op_params[0] = 1` on the three `mul_mat_id` nodes of each chain, see `src/llama-graph.cpp` `build_pack_chain`); the fork taught the CPU backend about it (`ggml/src/ggml-cpu/ggml-cpu.c:1698` writes a zero row and continues); the Vulkan backend reads `op_params[0]` as a precision flag, never checks ids, and evidently produces zeros for the out-of-range rows (outputs were correct at batch 33 and batch 512 with graph optimization off); SYCL validates the ids and aborts. `GGML_SYCL_ENABLE_OPT=0` does not help because the assert is before any optimized path. Not fixable from `scripts/`.
- **Source change that would be needed** (not applied, per the runbook): in the SYCL `mul_mat_id` row-mapping loop at line 4958, treat `row_id_i < 0` like the CPU backend does: skip it when counting `expert_counts` and building `routed_row_src`, and zero the corresponding `dst` row so the hot and cold outputs merge additively. Any other place in the SYCL `mul_mat_id` path that indexes experts by id needs the same guard. Small change, not verified here. With that, rows 5, 14 and 18 could be repeated with `BIN=$PWD/llama.cpp/build-sycl/bin`; given the stock SYCL numbers the cached SYCL configuration would likely beat the Vulkan one on decode.
- **Patch (added after the main run, at the user's request):** `scripts/patches/sycl-moe-cache-negative-ids.patch`, applied by `scripts/build.sh` for every build (idempotent via `git apply --reverse --check`). It mirrors the CPU backend's convention in the three places the SYCL `mul_mat_id` consumes ids: the counting sort for batched calls skips negative ids and returns the used-row count, the per-row fallback zeroes the output row for a negative id, and both fused GEMV kernels in `mmvq.cpp` write a zero row and return for a negative id. Rows routed outside the pack therefore contribute zeros and the hot and cold outputs merge additively. Measurements with the patched build are in the SYCL rows below when present.
- **With the patch the cache runs on SYCL and is correct** (rows 32 to 34): decode 10.9 to 11.1 tok/s, the best non-MTP decode of the run and 27 percent above Vulkan + cache; the probe matches the CPU reference within 0.03. Prefill does not get Vulkan's jump: 39 tok/s at `-ub 512` and 130 tok/s at `-ub 2048`, because SYCL's batched `mul_mat_id` is a loop of one GEMM per expert (about 73k launches per 512-token micro-batch) rather than Vulkan's single fused dispatch, and that loop, not the weight copies, is SYCL's prefill bound. MTP is a loss on SYCL.
- **So the two backends split the honours:** Vulkan + cache for prompt-heavy work (401 tok/s prefill, 8.6 decode), SYCL + patched cache for short-prompt chat (11 decode, 130 prefill). A second experiment, a single binary with both backends where SYCL owns the weights and the cache and Vulkan takes the offloaded prefill matmuls (`BACKEND=dual` in build.sh, `scripts/patches/sycl-offload-min-batch-env.patch`, run with `--device SYCL0,Vulkan0 -ts 1,0 GGML_SYCL_OP_OFFLOAD_MIN_BATCH=1000000`), is reported below.
- **Dual build**: `BACKEND=dual BUILD_DIR=build-dual scripts/build.sh` compiles both backends with icpx in 738 s; `llama-cli --list-devices` lists `SYCL0` and `Vulkan0` for the one card. With `--device SYCL0,Vulkan0 -ts 1,0` and `GGML_SYCL_OP_OFFLOAD_MIN_BATCH=1000000` the load log shows `SYCL0 model buffer size = 3816 MiB`, `4347.66 MiB uploaded to SYCL0`, `SYCL0 compute buffer size = 279 MiB` and `Vulkan0 compute buffer size = 799 MiB`, 218 graph splits: the weights, cache and decode are on SYCL, and the scheduler hands the offloaded prefill expert matmuls (and their weight copies) to Vulkan. Log `results/logs/dual-split-ub512.log`. The dual binary restricted to `--device SYCL0` reproduces the SYCL-only numbers (10.90 tok/s decode, `results/logs/dual-syclonly.log`), so the dual build itself costs nothing.
- **Measurement hygiene note for the dual runs:** the first dual-split server on port 8080 received, 0.3 s after start, a request that was not from the driver (4368-token prompt, `temperature 1.0`, `n_predict 32000`, seed -1) from a chat client still pointed at that port; it generated 12,016 tokens to the context limit and a second such request followed, so that pass was discarded (`results/logs/batch6-port8080-polluted.log`, `dual-split-ub512.log` from that pass) and the dual runs below were repeated on port 8089. Every earlier SYCL and Vulkan log was audited and contains only the driver's own requests.
- **Dual split, clean pass on port 8089 (`results/logs/batch6-split-garbage.log`): the offloaded path computes garbage.** Short-prompt decode is correct and coherent (9.40 tok/s, the think block reads normally, `results/bench/dual-split-ub512.txt`), but every request over the 32-token offload threshold returns nonsense: the 33-token probe generated `ph/////` with a flat 2 percent top probability and the 4759-token prompt's continuation is 64 slashes (`results/bench/dual-split-ub512.prefill1.json`). The speeds seen while producing that garbage, 97.8 tok/s at `-ub 512` and 136.6 tok/s at `-ub 1024`, are therefore not results, only an indication that the hot chain left on SYCL's per-expert GEMM loop would still cap the approach well below Vulkan's 401. Since the same cold-chain-on-Vulkan graph is correct in the Vulkan-only build and the SYCL-only build is correct with everything on SYCL, the fault is in the hand-off between two different GPU backends: the scheduler's cross-backend copies and event waits assume one backend family. Batch 7 tested the two switches that serialize those paths (`results/logs/batch7.log`):
  - 33-token probe, one micro-batch: `ASYNC_CPU=0` gives the correct token stream with `<think>` at 0.889 (CPU reference 0.926); `GGML_VK_DISABLE_ASYNC=1` alone 0.690; both 0.733. Three different values for what should be the same arithmetic already suggests a residual race between the two backends.
  - Measured with `ASYNC_CPU=0` (`split-ok-ub512`): short-prompt decode 9.77 tok/s, long prompt 103.5 tok/s, decode after it 9.05, but the long prompt's continuation is 64 slashes again (`results/bench/split-ok-ub512.prefill1.json`). So the async worker was one fault, and a second one remains for any request that spans more than one micro-batch: single micro-batch requests come out right, multi-micro-batch prompts come out as garbage. Batch 8 (`results/logs/batch8.log`, `split-b512`): with `-b 512 -ub 512`, one micro-batch per decode call, the long prompt still comes back as slashes (111 tok/s while doing so). So the defect is in the hand-off between the two GPU backends across prompt chunks, not in micro-batching within a call.
- **Dual-backend verdict: not usable.** The mechanism works as designed (decode on SYCL, offloaded prefill matmuls on Vulkan, both visible in the buffer placement and the split counts), and single-chunk requests are correct, but any prompt longer than one chunk is corrupted, and even correct probes vary run to run. The scheduler's cross-backend copies and event waits were only ever exercised with one backend family per process; making Vulkan and SYCL cooperate on one card would mean auditing `ggml_backend_sched_compute_splits` and both backends' `synchronize`/event implementations, which is beyond a run-time fix. The `BACKEND=dual` build option and `scripts/patches/sycl-offload-min-batch-env.patch` are kept for whoever picks that up. Even if fixed, the hot chain on SYCL's per-expert GEMM loop would cap prefill well under Vulkan's 401 (136 tok/s at `-ub 1024` while producing garbage).
- **Grouped MoE GEMV for SYCL (`scripts/patches/sycl-moe-grouped-mmvq.patch`, written 2026-09-13 at the user's request to close the prefill gap).** Adds a kernel that walks every routed (token, slot) row of a batched `mul_mat_id` in one launch (rows sorted by expert, activations quantized to Q8_1 once, -1 ids skipped) and adds IQ2_S/IQ4_NL to the single-token fused MoE GEMV. `test-backend-ops -o MUL_MAT_ID` on SYCL0 passes every case against the CPU backend with the new path (`results/logs/test-backend-ops-grouped.log`, 0 failures) and with the old path. Measured with the cache (rows 35 to 37): short-prompt decode 11.9 to 13.1 tok/s (from 10.9; the fused IQ path replaces 30 small launches per layer per token), but batched prefill is not faster: 41.6 tok/s at `-ub 512` (old path 39 to 51) and 73 tok/s at `-ub 2048` against the old path's 130, because at 40 rows per expert a per-row GEMV re-reads each expert's weights per row while the old dequantize-and-GEMM loop reuses them. The patch therefore uses the grouped GEMV only up to 8 rows per expert (`GGML_SYCL_MOE_GROUPED_MAX_ROWS`) and leaves large batches on the GEMM loop. The probe with the grouped path shows `<think>` 0.66 at position 1 (CPU 0.926, old SYCL path 0.895): same argmax, a larger rounding shift at that near-tie token, within the op test's tolerance.
- **Where SYCL prefill time actually goes.** The old path's 130 tok/s at `-ub 2048` is 15.9 s per 2048-token micro-batch; the scheduler copies roughly 40 GB of used cold experts into device memory per micro-batch, and Level Zero copies from pageable (mmap) host memory run at about 2.5 to 3 GB/s here, which alone accounts for that time. Vulkan writes its copies straight into host-visible VRAM over the Resizable BAR at near link speed, which is why it reaches 401. That hypothesis was tested and is wrong: the Level Zero copy-engine and staging knobs (`results/logs/batch11.log`) and a pinned double-buffered staging path in the backend's `set_tensor_async` (`results/logs/batch12.log`, 128 tok/s with it, 130 without) change nothing, and the GPU clock sits at about 2450 MHz during SYCL prefill against about 1900 during Vulkan's copy-bound prefill. SYCL's old batched path is launch-bound: one dequantize launch, one GEMM launch and a sync per expert, about 67,000 of each per 2048-token micro-batch. Three ways of removing those launches were then built and measured at `-ub 2048` (`results/logs/batch13*.log`, `batch14.log`): chunked dequantize plus oneMKL's group `gemm_batch` (83.7 tok/s, and a coarser rounding profile), chunked dequantize plus the per-expert GEMM call (110 tok/s), and chunked dequantize plus experts bucketed by row count into uniform pointer-array batched GEMMs, six launches per tensor (79 tok/s; `test-backend-ops -o MUL_MAT_ID` passes all 724 cases through it, `results/logs/test-backend-ops-bucketed.log`). All three lose to the old loop's 130, and the GPU clock is high in every one of them, so the launch count was never the limit either: **SYCL prefill on this model is bound by the expert GEMMs themselves, tall-skinny fp16 products (M 640 or 2560, K 2560 or 640, N 16 to 256 rows per expert) on dequantized weights, which oneMKL runs at a small fraction of the B580's peak.** Vulkan reaches 401 because its `mul_mat_id` is a fused integer-dot GEMM over the quantized weights with Q8_1 activations, and the SYCL backend has no such kernel (its `mmq`-style path exists only for dense `mul_mat` on a few types). Writing that kernel, an XMX int8 MoE GEMM with expert indirection, is the one change that would close the gap; it is a multi-day kernel project and was not attempted. The three experimental paths and the pinned staging upload stay in the patch behind opt-in env vars (`GGML_SYCL_MOE_BUCKETED_GEMM`, `GGML_SYCL_MOE_GROUPED_GEMM`, `GGML_SYCL_STAGED_UPLOAD`); the default remains the per-expert loop for batches and the fused GEMV for single tokens.
- **So "best of both worlds" on one backend is not available today.** Pick per workload: Vulkan + cache + `-ub 2048` for prompt-heavy use (401 tok/s prefill, 8.6 decode), SYCL + patched cache for short-prompt chat (11 decode, 130 prefill at `-ub 2048`). The lever that would change this is a fused batched `mul_mat_id` in the SYCL backend, which is what Vulkan has and SYCL lacks.
